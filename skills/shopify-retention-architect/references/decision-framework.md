# Retention decision framework

## Product repeat model

| Model | Primary mechanism | Loyalty role |
|---|---|---|
| Replenishable, predictable | Subscription | Tenure, status, referral, non-discount perks |
| Replenishable, variable | Lifecycle + loyalty | Reminder, progress, flexible rewards |
| Multi-SKU discovery | Loyalty/VIP | Identity, exploration, tier progress |
| Collectible/drop | VIP/community | Access, status, early drops |
| Durable/high-consideration | Referral | Advocacy, accessories, service |
| One-time/occasion | Referral or none | Do not force points |

## Stage evidence

### Foundation

- Too little history
- Product/fulfillment quality unresolved
- Customer identity or analytics unreliable

### Acquisition/conversion/AOV

- Repeat model may exist, but traffic/conversion/basket is the current fire
- First-order contribution cannot support CAC or incentives

### Second purchase

- Meaningful one-order population
- Product supports another purchase
- Weak post-purchase activation

### Subscription ready

- Same SKU/category repurchased
- Cadence is predictable enough to schedule
- Convenience has real value
- Inventory and fulfillment are dependable

### Loyalty ready

- Organic repeat already exists
- Multiple purchase occasions
- Margin funds rewards
- Active audience and touchpoints exist

### Community/VIP

- Strong repeat and customer value
- Brand affinity/status/access matter
- Benefits exist beyond discounts

## Economic rules

Never use a benchmark without naming its source and applicability.

- Cashback/reward cost should be derived from the merchant's margin and goal.
- Paid CAC and referral CAC must share the same customer qualification event.
- Nominal points issued are not cash cost; use expected redemption cost.
- Subscription discount and loyalty cashback can stack destructively; model combined cost.
- A tier threshold should be reachable by a deliberate share of customers, based on actual AOV/frequency—not round numbers.

## Creative program principles

- Currency should be countable and belong to the brand world.
- Tier names should describe identity/progression, not generic metals.
- Earning actions should use brand voice while mapping to real Joy events.
- The reward ladder needs reachable value and, where appropriate, one aspirational reward that cannot simply be purchased.
- Creative pages should feel like campaigns, but mechanics and economics must remain understandable.
